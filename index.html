The Lead · Security

Somebody Turned the Freezers Up to 50°C

Shops from Sweden to Estonia found their refrigeration tampered with. Danfoss says the Dutch installations ran on unprotected networks with default passwords, and that it warned customers on October 13.

TFTC, 2:30 p.m. EST Monday, with Cahlen, Calva and A.A.Ron

Overnight Sunday into Monday, somebody reached into the refrigeration of European shops and changed the numbers. TFTC reports that an ICA store in Sjöbo found its refrigerators and freezers set to 50°C, and that another ICA in Stockholm threw away a significant quantity of food. Retailers and suppliers also reported incidents in Belgium, Luxembourg, Ireland, the Netherlands and Estonia. Whether one attacker is behind all of it is not known. The BBC's headline this morning, on the wires below, puts it at ten countries.

The explanation so far is unglamorous. Danfoss told NOS that at least 17 Dutch businesses were affected, butchers and neighborhood shops among them, and that those installations ran on unprotected networks with default passwords. It had warned customers on October 13 to remove direct internet exposure.

The one good result comes from Estonia. A Selver store had its freezer set to 50°C too, but the company had prepared after the earlier attacks. Its partner responded within ten minutes, the temperature rose only two or three degrees, and no products were damaged.

TFTC's conclusion is an engineer's: The engineer designing the system should justify every outside connection, and the machine should keep doing its essential job when that connection disappears. Its advice to everyone else, with Sweden recommending at least a week of supplies at home, is shorter. Keep your pantry stocked. Harden everything.

The rest of your network was thinking along the same lines on Monday, without mentioning freezers. Cahlen recommended five-gallon buckets with a screw-top lid for storing dry food. Calva listed Survival LLM, an assistant and survival library that runs on a phone with no internet. And A.A.Ron, an electrical engineering subcontractor to the US Air Force from 2010 to 2020, recalled spare budget going to backup generators on boring civil projects like dorms and sewers: Too many of those guys have all read 1 Second After.

Exchanges

Mercado Bitcoin Stops Withdrawals After a 34-Minute Drain

Brazil's largest exchange suspended deposits and withdrawals on November 2 after wallets on at least 10 blockchains were drained, TFTC's news bot reports, four days after the $6.3 billion Binance hack. Most of the suspicious transfers fell between about 06:19 and 06:53 UTC.

Hypernative puts the outflows above $40 million. The Block, by way of Nora News, has the exchange confirming an incident of $46 million. Tether's chief executive says $4.5 million in USDT has been frozen.

The exchange says no customer funds were lost. It has not said which system was breached or how signing keys were obtained. Customers are currently taking the exchange's word for it, TFTC's bot writes.

The price was around $76,700 this morning, Nora News says, after a brief drop below $76,400, its lowest since August. BlackRock's IBIT lost $405.3 million on Monday, its biggest daily outflow since June 26.

Policy

Two Fronts Open Against DAC8

Bull Bitcoin is challenging the decree that writes the EU's crypto reporting directive into French law, before the Conseil d'État, and wants the directive itself referred to the EU Court of Justice.

Bitcoin Austria has now opened a second front: at its initiative, attorney Dr. Oliver Völkel has filed a complaint with the Austrian Data Protection Authority, on the same road that brought down EU data retention in 2014. DAC8 stands in all 27 member states, so Bull Bitcoin counts what's left: with two fronts now open, 25 remain. Source.
